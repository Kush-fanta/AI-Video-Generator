# Motion Design

Motion design governs how designed elements behave over time.

For Varnam, this is not generic animation polish. It is the craft of using movement, transitions, and reveal logic to make meaning clearer, sharper, and more memorable on screen.

## First Principles

1. **Animate change in meaning.** Motion should reveal, compare, escalate, punctuate, or reframe. If nothing becomes clearer, the motion is decorative.
2. **Use one motion language per sequence.** A sequence should feel internally coherent: draws, fades, pushes, pulses, swaps, or wipes should behave like one system.
3. **Preserve object constancy.** When the viewer needs to track a thing across change, keep that thing alive and mutate it instead of replacing it.
4. **Reveal in reading order.** Motion should reduce scan cost, not increase it.
5. **Transitions carry editorial logic.** Hard cut, dissolve, wipe, and fade through black each make a different claim about continuity.
6. **Hold after the payoff.** A reveal that disappears the moment it lands has not actually landed.
7. **Respect the screen's processing budget.** Too many simultaneous motions flatten hierarchy and make the frame unreadable.
8. **Use anchors instead of ghost stacking.** Keep one active thing alive across change when continuity matters; do not leave visual debris everywhere.
9. **Cycle modes deliberately.** Text, diagram, image, map, and data-viz should rotate with purpose instead of appearing as isolated interruptions.

## Motion Tests

### Meaning Test

What does this motion teach or emphasize that the static frame does not?

### Continuity Test

Can the viewer track the active object or payload through the change?

### Family Test

Does this sequence use a disciplined motion language, or a grab-bag of tricks?

### Hold Test

After the key change lands, is there enough stillness to read it?

## Failure Modes

Reject the motion plan if it becomes:
- animation for its own sake
- too many motion families in one beat
- transitions with no editorial reason
- reveals that force the eye to re-scan the frame
- constant movement that erases emphasis
