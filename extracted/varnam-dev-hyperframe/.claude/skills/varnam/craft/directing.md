# Directing

Directing is the governing craft that turns a topic into a film approach.

For Varnam, directing now lands in `direction/board/index.md`. It is the root canvas for the board: short, film-level, and strong enough that every scene board can answer to it. It is not a separate prose deliverable, not a beat list, and not a build manifest.

If the board index cannot guide scene boards, it is weak. If it already reads like a cut-by-cut board, it is in the wrong grain.

## Purpose

The board index exists to answer:
- what this piece is really saying
- what the script is doing beneath its literal words
- why it matters now
- who it is speaking to
- what variation of the channel identity this script calls for
- what story-specific design pack this film needs, before any runtime composition choice
- what viewing experience it should create
- what visual and sound systems carry that experience
- what this film must refuse

This is the root that keeps the scene canvases coherent before build pressure starts flattening them into generic components.

## First Principles

1. **Direction is authorship of proof.** It decides how the film will make the claim believable on screen and in sound.
2. **Point of view is a decision, not a mood word.** If the piece has no clear stance, the direction is weak.
3. **Systems beat adjectives.** Define how image, sound, rhythm, and evidence behave; do not hide behind words like `cinematic` or `premium`.
4. **Every mode needs a job.** If a visual or sound mode has no function, it is indulgence.
5. **Restraint is structural.** The film gets power from what it refuses to do, not from using every available trick.
6. **Constraints belong inside the direction.** Real-media obligations, factual limits, and credibility boundaries are part of the film design.
7. **Scene boards should inherit decisions, not vibes.** A good board index reduces guesswork downstream.
8. **Channel identity is a baseline, not a preset.** Each script gets a project-specific variation: colder, denser, more forensic, more lyrical, more abrasive, more restrained. The board index names that variation so the film does not become mono-channel wallpaper.

## What The Board Index Must Lock

### 1. Core Frame

Start with the basic frame of the piece:
- title or working title
- format
- runtime bracket
- target viewer
- desired viewer outcome

This is not metadata for its own sake. It defines the scale and pressure of every later decision.

### 2. Editorial Thesis

State:
- the core argument
- the point of view
- why now
- what the piece refuses to become

If this is fuzzy, the film will drift into summary, journalism-by-aggregation, or borrowed mood.

### 3. Narrative Spine

Describe the movement of the piece:
- opening pressure
- central turns
- what deepens or complicates the claim
- where the piece lands

This is still high level. It should describe the dramatic line, not scene inventory.

### 4. Channel Variation

Name how this film adapts the channel identity:
- temperature: colder, warmer, more clinical, more mythic, more confrontational
- rhythm: terse, accumulating, investigative, meditative, escalating
- proof mode: documents, mechanisms, people, place, scale, contradiction
- density: sparse pressure, dense evidence, alternating relief, deliberate stillness
- restraint: what the channel normally does that this script should avoid

The variation must come from the script and task brief, not from novelty for its own sake.

### 5. Visual System

Define the language the scene boards must spend:
- which evidence modes belong in this film
- what each mode is for
- what recurring motifs or visual throughlines matter
- how transitions behave
- what escalation looks like
- what kind of images are real, sourced, generated, diagrammed, or typographic

The board index should tell scene boards what kinds of proof exist on screen before they decide exact moments.

### 6. Per-Story Design Pack

The board index must translate the channel into a design pack for this story. This is the layer Claude Design got right: the story earns its own system, instead of selecting from a generic template shelf.

Lock:
- **visual modes:** painterly, archival, map, diagram, data, source-paper, typography, or other modes, and the story job for each
- **primitive set:** reusable visual/motion building blocks the story needs: grids, light streaks, connector lines, text reveals, coin/logo objects, UI frames, counters, document treatments, map markers, or other blocks
- **sprite pack:** movable visual objects: logos, cutouts, source frames, documents, phone screenshots, generated characters, recurring tokens, particles, or product objects
- **image language:** source/generated stance, grading, framing, negative space, focal rules, and recurring motifs
- **text grammar:** when text appears, what forms are allowed, and when silence/no text is stronger
- **edit grammar:** recurring beat types, transition logic, reveal patterns, mode-switch triggers, and escalation rhythm
- **sound grammar:** score bed, silence, diegetic/non-diegetic SFX, and where audio changes the argument
- **composition recipes:** likely reusable HyperFrames compositions, stated as story functions and primitive/sprite recipes rather than component names

Anti-pattern: "use hero card, stat card, timeline card" without explaining why those forms belong to this script. That is generic template picking. The board index should instead say what primitives and sprites recur, what they prove, and what design grammar carries them.

### 7. Sound System

Define:
- narration stance
- music role
- where silence matters
- whether audio is explanatory, emotional, ironic, or restrained at each major phase

The board index should make clear how sound supports the argument rather than decorating it.

### 8. Production Reality

Lock the constraints that materially shape the film:
- what must be factually verified
- what must use real media
- what cannot be shown loosely
- what permissions or evidence limits exist
- what would break credibility

If production reality is absent here, the board is pretending to be freer than the actual film can be.

### 9. Anti-Goals

Write down what the piece must not become:
- not a lecture
- not radio over wallpaper
- not generic "cinematic" mood
- not a research dump
- not a prompt aesthetic without editorial logic

Anti-goals keep scene boards from inheriting empty adjectives.

## What The Board Index Must Not Do

Do not use the board index to:
- write the full narration
- decide every beat
- list camera setups
- dump research
- write prompt packets
- hide vagueness behind words like `cinematic`, `premium`, `dark`, `epic`, or `emotional`

Every descriptive choice must imply an actual consequence for the scene boards.

## Shape Of The Root Canvas

Write it as compact prose. Present tense is usually strongest.

A good `direction/board/index.md` usually has five short sections:
1. argument and audience
2. narrative movement
3. channel variation
4. per-story design pack
5. visual system
6. sound system
7. constraints and anti-goals

References or styleframes are allowed only when they lock concrete behavior. They are not a mood-board appendix.

## Relationship To Scene Boards

The board index defines:
- what kind of film this is
- how it proves its claims
- how it should feel as it moves

Scene boards define:
- what the viewer sees and hears as each scene unfolds
- what arrives first
- how the scene turns
- what exact evidence or asset is required

The board index sets the system. Scene boards spend it.

## Acceptance Test

The board index is ready only if:
- the argument is clear
- the script interpretation is clear
- the channel variation is specific to this film
- the per-story design pack is specific to this story and not a generic component list
- the viewer outcome is clear
- the visual evidence strategy is clear
- the sound stance is clear
- the anti-goals are clear
- a scene board author could build from it without guessing the film's intent

## Common Failure Modes

Reject the board index if it:
- defines a topic, not a story
- makes claims without a visual proof plan
- has fuzzy POV
- collapses into script pages
- collapses into a shot list
- describes look with mood words only
- ignores feasibility or evidence reality
- carries too much backstory and too little film logic
