# Storytelling

Storytelling is the control layer above script, direction, and storyboard.

For Varnam, this doc exists to keep the system from confusing "having material" with "having a story." A story is not a topic, not a pile of facts, and not a mood. It is change under pressure that the viewer can track.

## First Principles

1. **A story is change under pressure.** If the state of the world, the argument, or the viewer's understanding does not change, there is no story yet.
2. **The viewer follows one active question at a time.** You can nest smaller questions, but there must be one dominant pressure line.
3. **Consequence is the engine.** Facts matter when they make something riskier, clearer, stranger, or more unavoidable.
4. **Escalation comes from new meaning, not more volume.** More examples, more numbers, or more footage are not escalation unless they change the claim.
5. **Causation beats sequence.** "What made this happen?" is stronger than "what happened next?"
6. **Specific human or physical reality grounds abstraction.** Systems land harder when they touch a face, a room, a machine, a document, or a decision.
7. **A turn is mandatory.** Good stories reverse, deepen, expose a hidden mechanism, or transfer agency.
8. **The ending must change the viewer's model.** A closing that only summarizes has not finished the work.

## Story Tests

### Change Test

What is more true at the end than at the beginning?

### Pressure Test

What question, risk, or unresolved contradiction keeps the viewer moving?

### Turn Test

Where does the piece stop being "more of the same" and become sharper, stranger, or harder to ignore?

### Causality Test

Can the audience explain why the major beats happened, not just remember that they happened?

### Human Ground Test

Where does the piece touch lived reality instead of staying in thesis-space?

## Failure Modes

Reject the story shape if it becomes:
- a topic tour
- a chronology with no argument
- a thesis with no turn
- a fact pile with no pressure line
- a mood piece with no causal spine
