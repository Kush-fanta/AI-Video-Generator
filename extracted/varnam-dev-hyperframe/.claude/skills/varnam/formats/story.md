# Story Format

Story-led documentary is the format where the viewer tracks change under pressure through a subject, not just through a thesis.

In Varnam, this is not channel taste. It is format doctrine for pieces that move by consequence, pursuit, discovery, and reveal.

## What This Format Is

Use story format when the film has a live spine the viewer can follow:
- a person under pressure
- an object with consequences attached to it
- a place that holds conflict, memory, or proof
- an unfolding event, investigation, search, collapse, or reversal

The viewer should be able to answer:
- what is changing
- what is at risk
- what is still hidden
- why the next beat matters

If the piece only needs to prove a claim cleanly, it may not be story format. It may be explainer.

## How It Differs From Argument-Led Explainer

Explainer format starts from the claim and organizes proof under it.

Story format starts from unstable reality and lets the deeper claim emerge through what happens, what gets uncovered, and what the consequence becomes.

Explainer can open with a sharp thesis.
Story usually opens with pressure, disturbance, or consequence before full interpretation.

Explainer can stay mostly abstract if the logic is clear.
Story must keep returning to concrete anchors: character, object, place, document, trace.

Explainer answers "how does this work?" or "what should I believe?"
Story answers "what happened here, why did it change, and what does that reveal?"

## Governing Rules

1. **Pressure first.** Open on strain, danger, contradiction, pursuit, or loss. Do not open with background lecture.
2. **Anchors are mandatory.** The story must keep landing on a character, object, place, or document the viewer can track.
3. **Consequence drives the cut.** A new beat earns its place when risk sharpens, meaning changes, or a hidden mechanism becomes visible.
4. **Reveals change interpretation.** A reveal is not extra information. It reorders what the earlier beats mean.
5. **Scenes earn abstraction.** System explanation comes after the viewer has something concrete to care about.
6. **Return to the anchor.** If the script leaves the tracked subject for too long, the piece drifts into essay.
7. **Ending changes the model.** The landing should reframe the whole path, not summarize the reporting.

## Scene Pressure And Reveal Pacing

Each section needs one active pressure line:
- what is being pursued
- what might be lost
- what remains uncertain
- what new evidence could change the story

Each scene or beat should do at least one of these:
- tighten consequence
- expose a new fact that changes the read
- move closer to the hidden mechanism
- return to the anchor with higher stakes

Reveal pacing rules:
- establish the anchor before the big interpretation
- let the viewer feel the gap before you close it
- do not spend the strongest reveal in setup
- do not stack three small facts and call it escalation
- once the reveal lands, show its consequence immediately

## Character, Object, And Place Anchoring

Story format needs a stable center of gravity.

Possible anchors:
- **Character:** the person carrying the cost, choice, witness, or obsession
- **Object:** the ship, file, machine, statue, drug, bridge, weapon, map, or artifact that concentrates consequence
- **Place:** the port, town, courtroom, factory, border, temple, neighborhood, archive, or ruin where the pressure becomes visible

Rules:
- name the anchor early
- show the anchor early
- revisit the anchor after each major reveal
- if the VO names the anchor, the board must surface it on screen
- if the anchor changes, mark the handoff explicitly

Without a stable anchor, narrative documentary collapses into moving exposition.

## What Script Must Do Differently

The script is not a chapter essay. It is a pressure path.

It must:
- introduce the anchor before long explanation
- state the consequence before broad context
- write sections around turns, not topic buckets
- carry unresolved questions long enough to matter
- delay full interpretation until the viewer has concrete footing
- return to the anchor when the film risks drifting abstract

The script must not:
- explain the whole system before the story has started
- front-load all names, dates, and backstory into the opening
- replace scenes with summary
- treat the reveal as a closing footnote

## What Storyboard Must Do Differently

The storyboard must preserve pursuit and orientation, not just visual variety.

It must:
- show where the story is physically or materially grounded
- board the anchor repeatedly, not once
- mark which beats are setup, search, reveal, and consequence
- show traces, aftermath, absence, geography, and evidence close-ups when those carry the story
- use transitions to uncover, compare, return, or recontextualize
- keep the viewer oriented in who, what, and where each reveal belongs to

The board must not:
- convert every beat into generic montage
- spend long stretches in abstract type while the story claims to be about a real subject
- reveal the answer before the scene has asked the question
- cut away from the anchor so long that the viewer forgets the thread

## What Task-Config Must Lock

For story format, `task-config.md` must lock more than thesis and vibe.

It must pin:
- **Format:** explicitly mark this as story-led / narrative documentary
- **Core message:** what the story ultimately proves, not just the topic
- **Why now:** the present consequence or urgency
- **Target viewer / runtime:** enough room for setup, reveal, and consequence
- **Key subjects:** the exact character, object, place, and institutions the story tracks
- **Exact facts / names / dates:** the anchors that cannot drift
- **Media / evidence obligations:** what real footage, documents, locations, or source images must appear for the anchor and the reveal
- **Script must:** opening pressure, anchor introduction, reveal line, and landing behavior
- **Storyboard must decide:** recurring anchor imagery, reveal beats, and consequence imagery
- **No-go moves:** lecture opening, floating abstraction, generic stock standing in for the anchor, ending in summary

If these are not locked in the brief, the package will slide back into explainer habits.

## Common Failure Modes

Reject the format execution if it becomes:
- **thesis-first explainer**: the argument arrives before the viewer has a story to hold
- **chronology without pressure**: things happen, but nothing feels at stake
- **anchor abandonment**: the person, object, or place appears once and vanishes
- **reveal without setup**: the film drops the answer before the question has weight
- **context swamp**: background takes over and the live thread dies
- **generic montage drift**: the board looks busy but no beat advances consequence
- **false escalation**: more facts, more words, more footage, no new meaning
- **summary ending**: the film explains what we learned instead of landing the consequence

## Acceptance Test

Story format is working only if:
- the viewer can name the anchor
- the viewer can feel what is under pressure
- the major reveal changes the read of earlier beats
- the consequence sharpens after the reveal
- the ending lands on changed understanding, not recap
