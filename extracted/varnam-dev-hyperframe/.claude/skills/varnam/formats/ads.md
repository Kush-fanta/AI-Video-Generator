# Ads

Ad-style pieces are compressed persuasion films.

In Varnam, this format exists to create desire, belief, and action under severe time pressure. It is not channel taste, not campaign strategy, and not a miniature explainer. It is a format doctrine for pieces whose job is to move the viewer from interruption to response.

## What This Format Is

An ad in Varnam is a short film with one commercial objective:
- make the viewer stop
- make the value legible fast
- prove the claim quickly enough to earn belief
- convert that belief into a clear next action

The unit of success is not "they understood the topic." The unit of success is "they felt the pull, believed the promise, and knew what to do next."

## What Success Looks Like On Screen

The screen should feel:
- immediate, not warmed up
- singular, not essay-like
- proof-carrying, not mood-only
- visually decisive, not exploratory
- directed toward action, not toward contemplation

By the end of the piece, the viewer should be able to answer all of these without effort:
- What is being offered?
- Why should I care right now?
- Why should I believe it?
- What do I do next?

If any answer is fuzzy, the ad is not finished.

## How This Differs From Long-Form Explainer

Long-form explainer earns attention through expanding understanding. Ads must spend attention immediately.

### Script Must Do Different Work

The ad script must:
- open on pressure, desire, pain, or payoff in the first line
- carry one promise, not a layered thesis
- compress context to the minimum needed for belief
- turn proof into momentum instead of pausing for exposition
- land on an offer or action, not on a reflective summary

The ad script must not:
- build a slow factual runway
- stack three arguments when one would convert
- defer the point until "after the setup"
- sound like narration pasted over a montage

### Storyboard Must Do Different Work

The ad storyboard must:
- reveal the offer early enough that the viewer is never asking "what is this?"
- show proof assets fast: product, result, user state change, metric, demo, comparison, testimonial, or consequence
- cut dead transitions and decorative scene-setting
- reserve screen time for the offer card and CTA landing
- design for first-read clarity under fast playback and partial attention

The board is not allowed to hide the commercial payload until the end.

## Default Retention Shape

Use this as the default ad shape unless the brief clearly requires another:

1. Hook
2. Immediate orientation
3. Proof escalation
4. Offer
5. CTA

In time terms:
- first 1-2 seconds: interrupt and frame the opportunity or pain
- early middle: establish what this is and why it matters
- middle: stack the strongest proof, not all available proof
- final stretch: state the offer plainly
- last beat: tell the viewer exactly what to do

Ads do not have a "context chapter." Context appears only when it sharpens conversion.

## Pacing, Compression, Proof, Offer, CTA

### Pacing

- Enter fast.
- Cut as soon as the read lands.
- Escalate every beat.
- Treat every extra second as a cost.

An ad can breathe, but it cannot drift.

### Compression

Compression does not mean speed-reading. It means each line and frame carries a job.

Cut anything that does not do one of these:
- stop attention
- clarify the promise
- prove the claim
- sharpen the offer
- direct the action

### Proof

Proof is mandatory. Ads fail when they ask for belief without earning it.

Use the strongest proof mode the piece can actually support:
- direct product or workflow demo
- concrete before/after
- quantified result with sourceable basis
- comparison that makes the difference visible
- testimonial or quote with specific claim
- real-world consequence shown on screen

Proof must arrive before the CTA asks for trust.

### Offer And CTA

The offer is the conversion payload. The CTA is the instruction.

The piece must state:
- what the viewer gets
- why now
- what action to take next

Weak CTAs usually come from weak offers. If the ending sounds generic, the brief is under-specified upstream.

## Task-Config Locks

For ad format, `task-config.md` must lock the commercial spine before authoring starts.

- `Core message`: one promise only. If it reads like a thesis paragraph, it is wrong.
- `Anti-goals`: explicitly ban explainer drift, mood-only montage, vague branding, and soft CTA endings.
- `Tone / emotional arc`: define the pressure curve: intrigue, urgency, credibility, aspiration, reassurance, or aggression. Do not leave the emotional strategy implicit.
- `Visual approach`: lock proof modes, product visibility, on-screen text density, and whether the piece sells through demo, claim, comparison, or transformation.
- `Key subjects`: name the offer object, the user state, the proof artifact, and the final action target. If the piece depends on a founder, customer, screen, device, pack shot, price card, or stat, list it.
- `Target length`: use an exact runtime bucket. Ads cannot be authored against an open-ended duration.
- `Research requirements`: lock claim verification, legal-sensitive lines, pricing accuracy, testimonial authenticity, and any number that appears on screen or in VO.

If these are not locked, the script will sprawl and the board will hide the sale.

## Common Failure Modes

Reject the ad when it becomes:
- **The mini-explainer**: too much setup, not enough conversion pressure
- **The mood film**: atmosphere present, offer missing
- **The claim stack**: too many benefits, no dominant promise
- **The proofless pitch**: asks for trust before showing evidence
- **The late reveal**: viewer spends half the runtime not knowing what is being sold
- **The generic CTA**: "learn more" energy with no specific reason to act
- **The fast mush**: compressed language but unclear first read
- **The pretty irrelevance**: strong frames that do not advance persuasion
- **The buried offer**: product, price, or next step appears too late or too small

## Acceptance Test

The format is working only if:
- the hook is legible immediately
- the offer is clear early
- the proof is stronger than the claim
- the ending gives one obvious next action
- no beat survives on style alone
