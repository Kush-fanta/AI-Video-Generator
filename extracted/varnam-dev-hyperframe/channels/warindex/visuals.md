# WarIndex - Visuals

## Core Principle: Build The Operations Room

The WarIndex frame should feel like a strategic board coming alive in the dark.

The world is rendered, not photographed. Geography, routes, tonnage, runway length, time windows, and force ratios are the primary visual language. The viewer should feel they are looking at a live reconstruction, not a documentary collage.

## Graphics-First Rule

This channel is overwhelmingly synthetic.

- **80-90% of runtime:** Remotion-built 2D and 2.5D graphics
- **0-20% of runtime:** archival or evidentiary media, only when it proves something the graphics cannot

If a real image appears, it appears as evidence. It is framed, labeled, and held briefly. It never takes over the visual identity.

## Visual World

Dark navy-black base. Steel-blue grid. Warm amber for India, successful routes, and decisive numbers. Cold white for labels. Muted red for threat, denial, and failure.

This is not cinematic darkness for mood alone. The dark canvas gives tactical lines, air corridors, runways, and map annotations a reason to glow.

## Visual Modes

The channel rotates through a tight family of modes:

1. **The globe or regional map** - the strategic board
2. **City-grid reconstruction** - streets, compounds, runways, ports, terminals
3. **Route trace** - air corridor, sea lane, insertion path, withdrawal path
4. **Constraint stack** - labeled cards for distance, reserves, forces, refusals, timing
5. **Hero ratio** - giant hard numbers or asymmetry pairings
6. **Verdict line** - a short text payload on a stripped frame
7. **Evidence insert** - one real still, document, or headline when reality must stamp the point

The mode family is narrow on purpose. Variety comes from transformation inside the tactical world, not from jumping into unrelated aesthetics.

## 2.5D, Not Game-Engine

WarIndex should feel dimensional without becoming a game cinematic.

Depth comes from:

- layered maps
- camera drift
- panel tilt
- fog and atmosphere
- glows, pulse rings, and distant particles
- labeled planes, ships, and nodes moving through space

Do not chase photoreal 3D. The goal is legible strategic depth, not simulation realism.

## Compositional Rules

**Every frame must answer one operational question.** Where is it? How far? How long? Who can move? What is blocked? What changed?

**Lead with the subject before the support system.** If the scene is about an operation, the operation name, route, or contested place must read first. If the scene is about a person, the person must read first. Dashboards, scorecards, and evidence panels only exist to support that subject, never to replace it.

**When the subject is force, show force.** Aircraft should land on decks. Armor should move through terrain. Smoke, dust, wake, sparks, and tracer lines should carry the beat. Never use interface chrome as a substitute for mass in motion.

**Form before treatment.** The shot must work as plain shape, motion, and contrast before any glow, mist, blur, sparks, or atmosphere are added. If the scene only works after effects, the scene is not ready.

**Early style probes stay singular.** For capability tests, one aircraft and one terrain layer is enough. A hand-drawn line plate or an image-led terrain plate is valid when the subject reads instantly and the motion stays deliberate.

**Prop proofs must be staged to read.** When validating a plane, ship, tank, or missile prop, the object must occupy enough of the frame to judge silhouette, scale, and contrast immediately. Do not validate props as tiny dark thumbnails against dark backgrounds.

**Center the board, not the decoration.** Main map, route, or tactical panel owns the frame. Secondary labels orbit it.

**Labels are terse and uppercase.** The visual language is briefing-room, not editorial-magazine.

**The map keeps returning.** A WarIndex video should revisit the same geography with added consequence. The map is not a cutaway. It is the argument's anchor.

## Motion

Motion should feel precise and mission-driven.

- route lines trace, they do not scribble
- rings pulse on meaningful nodes
- panels slide in on rails
- numbers lock with weight
- labels snap into place
- the camera drifts or pushes, never jitters

Hard cuts belong on decision turns and consequence lines. Most internal animation is smooth, controlled, and tactical.

## Text Treatment

Text is used in three distinct ways:

1. **Ops label** - small uppercase, tracking, contextual
2. **Constraint card** - medium weight, one line, high signal
3. **Verdict line** - large, sparse, final

Text should not explain what the map already explains. It should sharpen the board.

## Evidence Usage

Real media is allowed only when it upgrades trust:

- a satellite still
- a runway image
- a ship photograph
- a treaty document
- a news headline

When used, treat it as a forensic insert:

- framed
- labeled
- brief
- subordinate to the operations board

No lifestyle b-roll. No heroic slow motion. No presenter footage. No generic war footage wallpaper.

## What This Channel Does Not Do

- no parchment or editorial cream canvas
- no bgless cutouts on warm paper
- no full-bleed AI illustration
- no rainbow infographics
- no busy stock montage
- no live-action-first documentary grammar
- no decorative maps without operational labels
